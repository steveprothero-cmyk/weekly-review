Week to Saturday 7 November 2026 · 6 stories

Wall of Tears risk never rated above Medium, and a $21m deficit no one has priced

A cancelled gallery exhibition, a $21 million deficit, a $100 million roads ask with no roads named, a new public briefings committee, paid parking costs and Cr Casmiri's housing record.

This week I kept finding decisions made, or being made, with a key number or document missing. A gallery exhibition was cancelled for safety on a risk assessment that names no specific threat. A paid parking study was floated without running costs. A $100 million roads ask names no roads.

The money sits under all of it. The CEO has told forums Council is "unsustainable", and on the CFO's draft figures the deficit grows each year a decision is put off. Whoever is elected in September 2028 may inherit the bill.

There was some good news. Councillors voted 13 to 0 for a Briefings Committee the public can watch. My concern is who controls its agenda.

Pick of the week · Thursday 5 November
Andrew Constance Says Council Is “Unsustainable”.

The CEO has called Council "unsustainable". The deficit is $12.5 million this year and a draft $21 million next. There is word of no special rate variation this year, which I could not confirm. Closing the $21 million gap would take about 22%, roughly $380 a year on the average residential rate. I argue an administrator is not the likely outcome, because weak finances alone are not a listed ground.

Gallery decision · Monday 2 November

Wall of Tears: what Council’s own risk assessment says

Council cancelled the Wall of Tears exhibition, due at the Regional Gallery on 4 November, citing staff safety. Its 22 October risk assessment names no specific threat and rates every risk Medium or Low after controls, with most extra measures falling on organisers. Whether police were asked is unknown. I found no reported violence at other showings, and nothing I have seen shows the summit motion influenced the decision.

Council meetings · Tuesday 3 November

Shoalhaven’s new Briefings Committee will be open to the public. The CEO will decide what it covers.

All 13 councillors voted on 27 October for a Briefings Committee where staff brief councillors in public, with a livestream and minutes. I welcome it, but the CEO sets the agenda and can cancel on a day's notice. Three councillors must sign to add a topic, and development application call-ins and Questions on Notice are excluded. Council gave no cost estimate.

Paid parking · Wednesday 4 November

Paid parking: who benefits, who pays, and what is left?

Council is not yet deciding on paid parking. It surveyed residents on whether to spend about $150,000 on a business case, with the survey closing at midnight on 4 November. The report says operating costs have not been estimated. Published results elsewhere show Byron Bay kept about $84 of every $100 in 2023-24 and Nelson Bay about $59, while Brunswick Heads spent more than it raised in a part-year including set-up.

Roads funding · Friday 6 November

Council Wants $100 Million for Roads. What Happens When the Money Runs Out?

Council's advocacy prospectus asks the NSW Government for $100 million over four years for roads, about 36% of the $280 million urgent backlog. It names no roads, states no Council contribution and has no fallback. The $40 million local roads package pledged in April 2022 is now due for completion in March 2028. I found no documented link between the Mayor's conference networking and money for Council roads.

Housing · Saturday 7 November

Lower Costs, Lower Standards, No New Homes

I went through Cr Lou Casmiri's housing motions. Most lower costs for builders, and I found no motion requiring savings to reach buyers and no home delivered by anything he has moved. Council's own risk statement says the planning control changes may not lead to more affordable housing. Approvals run at 710 a year against a target near 980. Opinion is labelled, and no finding is made about his motives.
