$58 million in developer discounts, and Berry's sewer plant nearing capacity

Developer water and sewer discounts, a 7–6 pool vote, a conduct policy checked against the Acts, a reconciliation report with no targets, and a term of councillor votes counted.

This week I kept coming back to one question: what does it cost, and who pays? Council discounted $58.34 million of developer water and sewer charges over 11 and a half years, and staff now say Berry's sewer plant is nearing capacity with no funded fix.

The 27 October meeting gave me plenty to check. A pool opened early on a 7–6 vote after the costs report was deleted. A conduct policy was adopted with no vote against. A reconciliation report called all 48 actions complete without one target or date.

I also counted every recorded vote this term. The 7–6 splits are still there, but since June the majorities have started to shift. The next few meetings will show whether that holds.

Pick of the week · Tuesday 27 October
The Council gave away $58 million to developers, and now we have to find money to extend the Berry system.

Council's February 2025 report shows $58.34 million of $99.89 million in water and sewer charges was discounted for developers between July 2013 and December 2024, or 58.4 per cent. The figure is nominal, not certain lost cash. The law says Council "may" charge and required no discount. Council's own report links the lost revenue to a sewer fund more than $67 million in deficit.

Biodiversity · Saturday 24 October

Councils’ peak body says biodiversity rules are too easy to bypass.

LGNSW's draft submission makes 18 recommendations to tighten the State's proposed Biodiversity Assessment Method, including limits on desktop-only assessment and a bar on using pre-emptive clearing to claim impacts were avoided. I set that against Shoalhaven's record: a 7–5 vote in May refusing support for conservation rezoning at Brooman, and a 4:1 panel recommendation for Sealark Road, Callala Bay. In my view the Brooman stance weakens council's hand.

Conduct policy · Friday 30 October

Council Adopted a Conduct Policy. Parts of It Go Past the Law.

Council adopted its Unreasonable Customer Conduct Policy on 27 October with no vote against and no changes after submissions. Reading it against the Acts, I found no legislative source for seven provisions, including action over published comment, bans from open meetings and limits on formal GIPA applications. I am not a lawyer, I made one of the three submissions, and Council may read the law differently.

Voting record · Friday 30 October

How Shoalhaven councillors have voted this term

Across 47 meetings and 1,078 recorded votes this term, 315 were divided. The SIG four (Casmiri, Kemp, Proudfoot and Wilkins) won 77 per cent of divisions before June 2026, 67 per cent since, and 52 per cent in September and October, a small sample of 31. Mayor White and Cr Clancy have voted with them less often. The figures show what happened, not why.
